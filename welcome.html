What it is

Four jobs, one log

Most lifters run a plan in one app, a food diary in another, and a notes file for how it all actually felt. Zane is built on the idea that those are the same dataset, and that the interesting parts only show up when they sit next to each other.

Plan

Five ready-made splits, Wendler's 5/3/1 off your training max, or a guided setup that builds your own week. Fixed weekdays, a rotating cycle, or fully flexible.

Log

A lifting keypad built for chalky thumbs. Drop sets, myo-reps, lengthened partials and supersets you can link mid-workout, a plate calculator that knows which plates you own, and rest timers that reach you with the screen locked.

Track

Weight and steps in a daily log, with water and macros filling themselves in from their own trackers. Plus glucose, blood pressure, temperature, medications and cardio.

Coach

A coach writes plans, macro targets and protocols straight into your app, and can watch a session as it happens. What you logged, they read but never rewrite.

The part nobody else does

The plan reads the room

Zane asks how the muscle recovered, how the weight felt, and how big the pump was. Nothing to report? One button answers all of it. From that, your next session on that day gets its own set count and its own weight, landing on individual exercises and rotating fairly across the muscle group.

  • No manual math. No spreadsheet, no percentage tables, no guessing whether to add a set.
  • Three modes. Freeze your set counts and let feedback tune only the weight, run a fixed 4 to 8 week block with a weekly intensity ramp, or let both dials run open-ended.
  • It learns your ceiling. When a muscle first shows the overreaching signature, Zane remembers the set count where it happened and times the next block against it. The block ends when your body says so, not when the calendar does.
  • A rough day cannot cost you. Flag a session as rough and it softens the suggestion without touching your ceiling, and it does not count against you.

Health & nutrition

Everything around the barbell

Training is the easy half. The food tracker reads barcodes and nutrition labels, turns a typed description or a photo of your plate into an estimate you check before it lands, and remembers what you eat so logging it again is a tap and a confirm.

  • Meal planning that fills itself. Set your recurring meals once and the next seven days arrive already planned out, hour by hour. Tick each one off as you eat it, or change it first. Nothing counts until you do.
  • A shopping list from real data. Built from what you actually ate over the last two weeks and what you have planned, with stock tracking that flags whatever is running low.
  • Cook it, weigh it, log it. A guided cooking mode where you tick off ingredients, swap them mid-recipe, and weigh the finished dish. A portion becomes a real number instead of a guess.
  • The clinical stuff too. Glucose, blood pressure, body temperature, and a medication tracker with reminders and a weekly pillbox packing list.
  • A written read on yesterday. One tap turns the day into a paragraph: weight and its trend, macros against target, what you ate, what you trained and how it compared to last time.

Coaching

Bring your coach inside

Coaching in Zane is not a chat window bolted onto a log. Your coach invites you by email, you accept inside the app, and from then on they work with the real thing: your plans, your targets, your history.

  • They write, you own. Your coach builds plans, sets training-day and rest-day macro targets, and pushes meal and medication protocols into your app. What you actually logged, your sets, your weigh-ins, your check-in answers, they can read but never rewrite.
  • Check-ins every week. Your coach builds the form field by field. It arrives already filled in with your week of weight, macros, steps and cardio, and both sides scroll back through every one.
  • An AI read on each check-in. One tap turns a week of numbers into a coach-style paragraph on where the trend is heading. You and your coach see the same text.
  • Live spectating. While you are mid-workout your coach can drop into the session and follow it set by set, refreshing as your sets land.
  • Or coach yourself. The same dashboard, the same form builder, the same trend cards, pointed at your own data.

On your phone

Built for the gym floor

Thumb-sized keys and contrast tuned per theme, because a gym is not a desk. Four themes, ten accent colors, and your entire history exports to a single file you own.

  • Every set arrives pre-filled. Not from your last session, from your best of the recent ones, so one bad day does not set the target. Earn the jump and you get told about it.
  • Sign in with your face. Passkeys, Face ID, Touch ID or your device PIN. No password to type at the gym.
  • Rack the bar, pocket the phone. The rest timer reaches you with the screen locked and the app closed, and so do reminders for training, water, meals, medication and messages from your coach.
  • Offline once installed. A full workout logs with no signal and syncs itself the moment you are back. Only the first sign-in needs a connection.

iPhone

  1. Open zane-wo.com in Safari. Chrome and Firefox cannot install apps on iPhone.
  2. Tap the Share button, the square with an arrow pointing up. Depending on your iOS version it sits in the bottom toolbar or in the address bar menu.
  3. Scroll through the share sheet and pick Add to Home Screen. If you do not see it, keep scrolling.
  4. Confirm the name and tap Add in the top right corner.

Android

  1. Open zane-wo.com in Chrome.
  2. Tap the three-dot menu in the top right corner.
  3. Pick Add to Home screen. Some Chrome versions call it Install app.
  4. A dialog appears with the Zane icon. Tap Install, and it opens like a native app from then on.

On iPhone and iPad, push notifications only work once Zane is installed this way and switched on in Settings.
